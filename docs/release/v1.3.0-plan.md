# Jura Trace v1.3.0 release plan

**Target: Friday 19 March 2027.** The arithmetic is in "Sequence and the
date". Argue with that section first.

**Status: draft, 5 November 2026.** Written at Paul's request ("onto
version 1.3.0"). Two decisions taken at the terminal the same day:
watermarking is the headline feature, and the plan proposes the date
rather than being given one.

**What this draft is, and is not.** The v1.2.0 plan could say that every
estimate carried the document that produced it, because three deep dives
measured its largest items. This plan cannot. Of the 45 backlog items
marked "Target v1.3.0", one carries an effort figure (BL-WM-001), and that
figure was written for a route the 22 October rescope abandoned. **Every
other number in the scope table is this plan's own estimate.** The
checkpoint on Thursday 28 January exists to replace them with measured
ones, and the watermarking spike gate on Friday 27 November exists to
replace the largest one much earlier.

**Verified against** `main` at `be91f235` on 5 November 2026, the
backlog README and item files, the live post at
`juralabs.org/updates/jura-trace-1-2-0-and-whats-next` (modified 3 November),
crates.io for `c2pa` and `trustmark`, and the brain review
`inbox/2026-10-22-trace-v130-review.md`.

## What v1.3.0 is for

v1.2.0 gave Trace a headless API, a signed Verification Record and a
smaller, quicker install. v1.3.0 has two jobs, and the second is the one
that cannot be skipped.

The first is what the public has been told. The live 1.2.0 post promises
four things for 1.3.0: retraining on 2026 image generators, reverse image
lookup that also tells you when Content Credentials have been stripped,
better feedback on why a result is uncertain, and PDF analysis. Paul has
added a fifth as the headline: an invisible watermark that survives the
recompression, resizing and cropping that strip a manifest, declared in the
manifest as a C2PA soft binding so it can be found again.

The second is that Trace has to keep being right about Content
Credentials. It holds a C2PA Validator conformance record. Its trust lists
are six months old, it cannot say which trust list a signer is on, the
library it validates with is about to delete APIs nobody has counted, and a
published attack has not been run against it. Six backlog items are rated
Critical and four of them are C2PA. None of them is visible as a feature,
and all of them land before any of the five headlines.

**No feature costs Jura Labs anything to run.** Checked in the code on
5 November 2026.

- **B3, reverse image search, runs only on the user's own key.** The only
  key source is the operating system's key store, filled by the user in
  Settings (`src-tauri/src/ris.rs:159-196`). No Jura Labs key exists in
  the code, and BL-RIS-001 forbids shipping one. With no key saved, there
  is no search. Every search also needs the user's confirmation before the
  image is sent (`ris.rs:904`), to Google under the user's own account.
- **B4, Watched Locations, makes no paid calls.** It fetches the pages the
  user lists, over the user's own connection, and reads their Content
  Credentials. It does nothing until the user adds a page. The Community
  question is a count of watched pages, not a cost.
- **Enhanced network mode is the default** (`network_mode.rs:5`, changed
  25 April 2026), so "Enhanced mode only" does not mean off by default.
  What keeps both features from running unasked is the user's own key and
  the user's own list. The unpublished 24 October preview draft says reverse
  image search runs "only when you have turned on network access"; that
  line is wrong and must change before the draft is published.

**The B1 spike, and its gate on Friday 27 November.** BL-WM-001's whole
history is a bundle-size surprise: `imwatermark` pulled torch in past
`excludes` and doubled the bundle to 1.5 GB. The `trustmark` crate exists
(0.4.0, MIT, 29 September 2026). Before any build work the spike
establishes, in writing:

1. What the crate pulls in. If it runs its models through `ort`, Trace
   either gains a second ONNX Runtime in the Rust binary or calls the
   sidecar's (onnxruntime 1.24.4). Either is a decision, not a detail.
2. The licence of the model weights, separately from the licence of the
   code.
3. The bundle delta on macOS, Windows and Linux, from a CI build, not an
   estimate.
4. TrustMark-Q's survival at JPEG Q70, a 75% resize, a 20% crop and a
   simulated screenshot, on our own 20 images, before anything is wired in.
   The 0.999 figure at Q75 is from the review's reading of arXiv:2610.04151
   and has not been reproduced here.

**Pass:** weights usable commercially, bundle delta under 100 MB on every
platform, and survival at Q70 materially better than today's 0%. **Fail:**
B1 leaves v1.3.0. The pages that claim watermarking are corrected instead,
which BL-WM-001 costs at an afternoon, and the work moves to v1.4.0 with the
spike's findings attached. The headline does not get to eat the release.

**Why B2 needs calendar time, not working time.** The current-phone slice
is photos from real people who give permission for evaluation and for
training, including AI zoom and object-removal edits. That cannot be
compressed by working harder. The requests go out in week 1; if fewer than
enough photos are in by the checkpoint, B2 degrades to "measure and
publish" at the checkpoint rather than at the gate.

**Why B1 goes before B2 in December.** B1 is the larger engineering risk
and the spike will have just measured it. B2's December work is
generation, which runs unattended.

## The cut order, and the checkpoint that applies it

Applied at the checkpoint on Thursday 28 January, and again at any point
the pessimistic case becomes the likely one. This is the order to argue
with; it is a recommendation, and the decision is Paul's.

1. **Tier C**, all of it.
2. **R1, the text watermark investigation.**
3. **B4, Watched Locations alerts.** It needs a scheduler outside the Pro
   tier, which makes it the least ready of the five. The public post pairs it with reverse image lookup in one
   line, so B3 alone still answers that line in part.
4. **B5, PDF analysis.** Promised publicly, self-contained, easy to ship
   in a v1.3.1.
5. **B2 degrades, it is not cut.** v1.3.0 ships the current model and
   publishes the 2026 measurements, whatever they show. Both BL-DET-001 and
   the public copy already say a new model ships only if it passes its
   gates, so this outcome is honest.
6. **B3, reverse image search.**
7. **B1, watermarking**, last, because Paul chose it as the headline. It
   can still leave earlier through the spike gate.

**Checkpoint questions, 28 January.** Are A1 to A4 merged? Did the spike
pass, and is B1 inside its range? How many phone photos are in? Replace
every estimate in the scope table with what has been measured, and cut
until the pessimistic case fits.

## The release gate

The v1.2.0 gate (`docs/release/v1.2.0-plan.md`, "The release gate") holds
in full, with these additions:

- The 217-image golden set captured fresh from the release candidate and
  compared frozen app to frozen app. Every changed verdict is explained by a
  named change (A3, A5, B2).
- The C2PA conformance vectors pass on the release build, and the trust
  list counts in `trust-list/README.md` match the vendored files.
- The two A4 exclusion files give the result recorded in A4.
- If B1 ships: the robustness report re-run on the release build, with the
  same degradations as March 2026, and the UI's stated envelope matches it.
  An image signed with a soft binding, stripped of its manifest and
  recompressed at Q75, is found again.
- If B2's model ships: its three gates passed and are written in the model
  card. If not: the measurements are in the model card anyway.
- The release notes have a "Known limitations" section.

## What is explicitly out, and why

- **AI text detection and text watermark checks, as code.** Statistical
  text detection is out on false-positive evidence (human-text false
  positives from 0 to 100% across 13 detectors). Text watermarks are
  investigated in R1 and not implemented (Paul, 5 November).
- **PDF signing.** The library cannot do it yet.
- **Audio and video.** The post already says "in the future".
- **Live trust-list fetch at run time.** Local-first; if ever, Enhanced
  mode only (BL-C2PA-001).
- **OCSP revocation** (BL-C2PA-005). Moves to v1.4.0. A5 makes the panel
  say "not checked" instead.
- **c2pa 0.92 or later.** Pinned to 0.91.x. A1 removes deprecated calls so
  the move is small when it comes.

## Risks, ranked

1. **TrustMark brings a bundle or runtime surprise.** Mitigation: the spike
   gate, with the fallback written in.
2. **B2 fails its gates a second time.** The June retrain did. Mitigation:
   the degraded outcome is already the public promise.
3. **The phone slice does not fill.** Mitigation: requests in week 1, and
   the decision moves to the checkpoint.
4. **A4 shows Trace accepts a signature over nothing.** Then A4 grows by 2
   to 3 days for the warning and the trust-score change, and a report to the
   C2PA conformance contacts. The test files stay in the repository until
   the C2PA responds.
5. **A1 finds more deprecated calls than expected**, or 0.92 lands before
   A1 does. Mitigation: it is first in the sequence.
6. **Five headlines.** The pull to move the date instead of cutting is the
   failure the v1.1.0 plan records. The cut order exists so the checkpoint
   does not have to invent one.

- **BL-C2PA-003 names 0.91.4 as the next version.** crates.io had 0.91.11
  on 5 November. A1 targets 0.91.11.
